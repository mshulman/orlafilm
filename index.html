Eoghan Walls is an award-winning poet and author from Derry, Northern Ireland. His critically acclaimed debut novel, The Gospel of Orla (Seven Stories Press), serves as the source material for the film adaptation.

STORY

Orla is anguished after the recent death of her mother. Unable to connect with her grieving father or her friends, she secretly decides to journey to Northern Ireland to live with her mother’s family. Sneaking away on her bike along the canals and back roads of Lancashire, determined to catch a ferry across the Irish Sea, Orla’s escape is disrupted by a chance encounter with an enigmatic stranger wandering the towpaths.

This traveller is unlike anyone she has ever known. Witnessing him perform quiet, unexplainable feats that seem to defy death itself, an audacious conviction takes root in Orla’s heart: if this man possesses the power to bend reality, perhaps he can bring her mother back. What begins as a solitary flight transforms into an extraordinary pilgrimage through misty waterways, hidden sanctuaries, and a travelling circus—where a tender bond with Fantasmo, a soulful elephant whose sorrowful gaze mirrors her own, reminds Orla she is not alone. Bound by grief and cloaked by the dark of night, Orla and Fantasmo escape toward the coast.

On the windswept shores of Morecambe Bay, as her frantic father scours the coast, the journey races toward a confrontation where land meets the sea. Here, the boundary between miraculous hope and heartbreaking reality collides. Orla must confront the question: whether to hold on to the dead, or find the courage to heal and make her way home.

THEMES

GRIEF | CONNECTION | REDEMPTION

ORLA explores how grief isolates us when loss shifts the ground beneath our feet. Through one girl's collision with the inexplicable, an extraordinary bond pulls her back from the edge—asking the ultimate question: when reality is radically altered, how do we find the courage to make our way back home?

Eoghan Walls is a poet and author from Derry, Northern Ireland. His most recent poetry collection – Pigeon Songs (Seren, 2019) – was a runner up for the Pigott Prize, Ireland's largest poetry prize. The Gospel of Orla (Seven Stories Press and Seven Stories Press UK, 2023) is his first novel.

He currently lectures in Creative Writing at Lancaster University, where he lives with his wife and a small contingency of daughters who serves as his best critics.

Seven Stories Press was founded by publisher Dan Simon in 1995 with its British arm, Seven Stories Press UK, following in 2016. The press publishes a select list of international authors of fiction, non-fiction, and children’s titles, informed by its belief in the radical imagination and the power of books to change our DNA and the contours of the world around us.

Seven Stories Press Inc. is best known globally as a nexus for passionate voices of conscience and writers of the imagination, including being the first English-language publisher of the 2022 Nobel Literature laureate Annie Ernaux.
