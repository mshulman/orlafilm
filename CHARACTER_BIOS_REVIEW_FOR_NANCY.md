# ORLA — Character Bios Editorial Review
**Prepared for:** Nancy Garvin  
**Date:** 13 October 2026  
**Project:** *ORLA* Feature Film Adaptation (Well Produced Films, LLC)

---

### Editorial Purpose
Reviewing the extended back-of-card copy for the 6 character cards on the pitch website. The goal is authentic UK cadence, removing tautologies and screenplay jargon, and ensuring poignant emotional resonance for British and international co-producers.

---

## Comparison Table: Current vs Proposed British English Revisions

| Character | Current Staging Copy | Proposed British English Revision | Editorial Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Orla**<br>*(Protagonist, 14)* | Orla is a bright but deeply troubled 14-year-old girl who has recently lost her mother. She finds it impossible to settle into her home and school life with this new reality. She believes if she can escape to Ireland, her birthplace, she will find comfort and solace with her relatives. She works day and night to put everything in place for her journey. | **Orla is a bright but deeply troubled 14-year-old girl who has recently lost her mother. Unable to accept life without her, she finds it impossible to settle back into family and school. Convinced that escaping to Ireland, her birthplace, will bring her solace among her mother’s relatives, she schemes day and night to prepare for the secret journey.** | Replaces corporate phrase *"with this new reality"*; eliminates *"comfort and solace"* tautology; tightens rhythm from passive *"works day and night"* to active, evocative *"schemes day and night to prepare for the secret journey"*. |
| **The Stranger**<br>*(The Miracle-Worker)* | The Stranger is a refugee in his early 30s. He recently arrived in the county after being the sole survivor of a terrible accident at sea. A stranger in a strange land who came ashore with a special mission, he is searching for his community and his audience. Orla believes that she sees him perform miracles. | **The Stranger is a refugee in his early thirties. He recently arrived in the county after being the sole survivor of a catastrophe at sea. An enigmatic wanderer who came ashore with a special mission, he is searching for his audience. Orla believes that she sees him perform miracles and comes to believe that he can perform one for her.** | Uses British literary number style (*"early thirties"*); eliminates triple repetition of *"stranger"*; elevates dramatic stakes connecting his miracles directly to Orla's longing for healing. |
| **Orla’s Dad**<br>*(Grieving Father)* | Dad is barely holding it together. Recently widowed and grieving deeply, he has the responsibility of caring for his two daughters, Orla, 14, and Lily. By day he is trying to be a good father, but by night the sadness moves in and he drinks to ease his profound sorrow. | **Dad is barely holding it together. Recently widowed and grieving deeply, he struggles with the weight of raising his two young daughters, Orla and Lily. By day he tries to be a good father, but by night grief closes in and he drinks to dull the pain.** | Removes repetitive *"Dad... Dad"*; eliminates jarring comma clutter (*", Orla, 14, and Lily"*); harmonises tense (*"tries"* vs *"drinks"*); replaces melodramatic *"ease his profound sorrow"* with poignant, authentic British idiom *"dull the pain"*. |
| **Lily**<br>*(Younger Sister)* | Lily is the bridge between Orla and Dad. Although they are completely at odds with one another, they both agree that Lily needs their loving care. | **Lily is the gentle bridge between Orla and Dad. While sister and father remain painfully at odds, their shared devotion to Lily is the one bond that still holds the fractured family together.** | Shifts from transactional / custody-agreement phrasing (*"they both agree that Lily needs their loving care"*) to powerful emotional stakes that anchor Lily as the family's fragile emotional glue. |
| **Orla’s Mum**<br>*(In Memory)* | Mum comes to the story only through Orla’s memory of her. She feeds Orla’s internal conflict and ultimately becomes the inspiration for Orla’s redemption. | **Mum lives on in Orla’s memories. The ache of her absence drives Orla’s frantic quest, yet her remembered love ultimately becomes the key to her daughter’s healing.** | Replaces cold script-coverage jargon (*"feeds internal conflict"*, *"comes to the story"*) with resonant narrative prose centering on grief, memory, and healing. |
| **Fantasmo the Elephant**<br>*(Kinship & Freedom)* | Fantasmo becomes a magical presence in Orla’s journey, offering silent kinship through eyes that reflect the girl’s own sorrow. Bound by captivity, she longs to be liberated from the chains that bind her. | **Fantasmo becomes a magical presence in Orla’s journey, offering silent kinship through eyes that mirror the girl’s own sorrow. Trapped in captivity, she yearns to break free from the heavy chains that hold her.** | Resolves root-verb tautology (*"Bound... chains that bind her"* &rarr; *"Trapped... heavy chains that hold her"*); syncs card title so back matches front (*"Fantasmo the Elephant"*). |
